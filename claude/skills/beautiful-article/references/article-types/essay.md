# Article Type · essay

Opinion, commentary, narrative, reviews (products / books / papers / films), personal essays, columns, manifestos. **Opinion-driven** nonfiction writing.

- **Recommended retention**: 60-80% (keep the chain of argument and the strongest evidence; cut tangents, long-winded quotations, and repeated rephrasings).
- **Typical structure**: `Hero` (a headline with strong character) → `Lead` (**pose tension / contradiction / a counterintuitive question** to hook the reader) → `Section`: opening → argument → evidence → turn → closing. The structure **serves narrative pacing and need not be tidy or symmetrical** — a good essay can be 3 sections or 7, depending on rhythm.
- **Component choice**: prose is the **absolute lead**; `Quote` for citations / verbatim statements / opposing views; `Aside tone="aside"` for side notes / personal commentary / the counter-position; **use data components like `Table` / `RiskList` sparingly** (they break the narrative feel); use `Summary` only for long essays.
- **Raw boundary**: occasionally one expressive visual pause (a pacing graphic / conceptual contrast / emotional-arc curve) that serves the narrative rather than explaining a mechanism; low density — an essay typically only needs 1-3 Raw blocks.
- **Imagery tendency**: `press`-style warm editorial photography / illustration / manuscript pages; serves the mood but **doesn't upstage the prose**. A review-style essay can use `user-assets` (product / book / film screenshots).
- **Theme tendency**: `press` (editorial/publishing style), `bodoni` (column / feature), `andy` (soft narrative), `sottsass` (lively cultural commentary).
- **Self-check**:
  - Is there **one clear line of argument / narrative**? Can a reader restate the piece's core point in a single sentence?
  - Is the evidence strong? Are there at least 2 concrete examples (rather than vague assertions)?
  - Is the tone consistent and does it read like **it was written by a person** (not AI-flavored, not formulaic)?
  - Does the Lead really "pose tension," rather than lukewarmly laying out background?
  - Does the closing leave the reader with something (not necessarily a conclusion — it can be a new question / new perspective)?

> **When not to use essay**:
>
> - Reviewing an **engineering artifact** (PR / proposal / incident) → `review`.
> - Helping the reader make a decision → `briefing`.
> - Clearly explaining a mechanism → `explainer`.
> - It's a compiled dialogue / interview → `dialogue`.
